# Ground material design decisions

Use this process to choose the initial direction of net-new work or a significant
visual redesign, and for later material choices not settled by the user or
permitted project or Figma evidence. Skip exact reproduction, mechanical edits,
and choices already grounded by inspected evidence. Its purpose is better
judgment, not a research report or proof for every design detail.

## Isolate the uncertainty

Before research:

1. Inventory available requirements and evidence.
2. Name only unresolved decisions that could materially change the result.
3. Separate missing evidence from low-consequence choices that can be stated as
   assumptions.

Research the decision, not its broad category. Do not promote remembered
conventions, tool affordances, or skill examples into requirements. A broad
brief assigns the direction to the designer; it does not justify a generic
default. Apply suitable expertise, then seek evidence where materially different
interpretations remain plausible.

For a net-new product interface or significant visual redesign without an
established system, the first-write evidence gate is one opened actual product
artifact. Open or capture a rendered screen at useful scale, or inspect
permitted implementation code when it directly reveals layout or interaction.
Search tiles, snippets, prose, and memory do not pass this gate. If one access
route fails, use another permitted route or another artifact; neither visual
synthesis nor authoring substitutes for missing pixels or code. Treat the
artifact as evidence only for what it shows, then make an independent design
decision rather than reproducing it.

## Use expertise without inheriting a template

Use suitable product, interaction, visual, accessibility, or domain expertise
when the task needs it and the host makes it available. Inspect the selected
skill's complete instructions and retain only questions or constraints that fit
this platform, medium, and deliverable. Do not inherit an auxiliary skill's
default page anatomy, aesthetic, component grammar, media or motion expectation,
or quota: none is product evidence without independent support from the brief or
inspected artifacts. Let those sources and situated synthesis decide how the
product should feel. If complementary expertise is unavailable, proceed from
them instead of inventing a generic substitute.

## Obtain sufficient evidence

Choose the nearest credible source for the decision. Project and current-file
evidence has the closest context; primary guidance can establish requirements;
inspected products and artifacts can establish precedent. Search pages,
image-result tiles, snippets, metadata, and remembered summaries only locate
candidates. Open the actual screen, implementation, or specification. When
appearance matters, emit or open rendered pixels; when code is the evidence,
inspect the concrete structure, styles, assets, or interaction rather than
repository prose.

For named lineage, attribution, or system history, prefer primary, official,
creator, or institutional evidence when reasonably available. For interaction,
inspect a real product screen, demo, or specification that shows the relevant
behavior and state.

Before authoring, close behavior and visual expression as separate questions.
Functional precedent or a task-specific working object does not by itself settle
expression. Ask whether the proposed visible language could move unchanged to
an unrelated product after its content and working object are replaced. If so,
inspect an adjacent expressive artifact or use the bounded visual-synthesis step
below before deciding. Familiar choices remain valid when the context explains
them; translate relationships and principles rather than motifs.

Use no source quota. Stop when the remaining material uncertainty can be resolved
with a defensible design judgment. Do not research routine spacing, typography,
or familiar control anatomy merely to complete a checklist. If a consequential
choice remains genuinely unresolved, continue bounded research or ask; otherwise
make and retain the low-consequence assumption.

## Externalize unresolved visual synthesis

Inspected references can leave several materially different compositions
plausible. When that uncertainty remains after research and a capable
image-generation tool is available, one bounded pass with that tool may
externalize a small contrastive set of refined screen-level hypotheses. Open the
pixels and use them to clarify the working-object relationship, hierarchy,
rhythm, media, material, or interaction treatment; discard the pass when it adds
no useful decision.

Generated concepts are speculative sketches, not product evidence, a benchmark
to copy, or a flattened Figma deliverable. Do not trust their text or data or
trace their motifs literally. Skip generation when inspected evidence and
situated judgment already resolve the direction. A generated subject selected
as actual content becomes a separate asset role governed by `visual-assets.md`.

## Retain only useful reasoning

Keep enough private rationale to remember which evidence or assumption changed a
material decision and what the rendered result must demonstrate. Use no fixed
template, per-source ceremony, mood board, or user-facing research report unless
the task asks for one.

## Apply and verify

Translate the decisions into one coherent composition and judge the rendering
against the decided direction. Correct concrete mismatches; research or tune
further only for remaining material uncertainty or an observed defect.
