# Compose a product interface

Use this reference for net-new screens and flows without an established
composition. It describes relationships to resolve and inspect, not a preferred
visual style. The brief, evidence, and situated judgment determine the answer.

## Organize the product experience

Begin with what the person is trying to understand or change, the content that
carries that work, the states that alter its meaning, and the actions those
states invite. Decide what must remain persistent, what can recede, and what
changes across the flow. Let the application structure follow that work rather
than a remembered screen template.

Challenge the first plausible composition. If an unrelated product could use
the same major regions and state-action relationship after replacing its nouns,
revisit the load-bearing relationship. Do not compensate with branding,
decoration, or novelty. Familiar structures remain valid when they materially
help people understand and act on this product.

Treat the viewport as an inspection context, not the product boundary. Use
natural vertical extent for continuing content, a fixed shell with owned
scrolling regions for persistent workspaces, or a hybrid when the behavior needs
both. Density, control size, and interaction economy should follow the frequency,
precision, environment, and platform of the work.

## Shape one attention system

Decide what deserves attention now, what supports it, and what can remain
available without competing. Treat position, scale, typography, color,
contrast, imagery, motion, depth, and space as one system. A distinctive choice
spends attention; its strength and frequency should follow the importance or
meaning it carries.

Give like roles a coherent language and unlike roles enough distinction to be
understood. Boundaries, surfaces, and effects should clarify grouping, state,
behavior, or expression rather than merely fill regions. This does not
prescribe palette, geometry, density, or material language.

Spacing expresses relationships. Related content should feel intentionally
related; a change in level or role should produce a perceptible change in
rhythm. Judge repeated edges, baselines, seams, nested insets, and optical
alignment in rendered pixels. Nominal gap or padding values do not prove that
the visible spacing works.

## Choose truthful representations

Choose text, icons, images, illustration, data visualization, diagrams, and
native controls by the recognition, comparison, manipulation, or expression
the task requires. None is mandatory, and none is a default merely because it
is easy to serialize.

Use familiar iconographic affordances when they improve recognition, scanning,
or interaction economy; use text when its precise meaning is clearer. Do not
turn sourcing effort into a design rationale, and do not add icons by quota.
When inspected evidence establishes a compact or icon-led interaction
vocabulary, preserve its recognition and interaction economy unless the new
situation gives a product reason to change it. Replacing such a role with text
is a design change, not a neutral fallback. Route selected icon roles through
`visual-assets.md`.

Treat content-bearing media and visualization as part of the working object,
not decoration. Preserve the distinctions people rely on in a photo, product,
person, place, media item, chart, map, waveform, notation, or domain instrument.
Primitives may truthfully encode interface structure, state, data, or an
explicitly schematic diagram; they may not impersonate meaningful content.
Route selected asset roles through `visual-assets.md`.

## Experience before dissecting

Open the first materially complete screen before expanding the flow. Encounter
it at realistic scale and ask whether it feels considered for this situation
and helps someone perform the central task. Do not begin by scoring individual
properties.

If the whole is weak or contradictory, identify the moment where the intended
experience breaks, then inspect only the particulars needed to understand why.
The cause may be product structure, attention, representation, application
behavior, rhythm, or execution. Correct the relationship or system that owns the
problem instead of decorating over it or mechanically reversing a visible
motif.

When the whole is coherent, stop polishing merely to satisfy a checklist.

## Propagate the resolved system

Treat the representative composition as a contract for the requested flow.
Carry its relevant hierarchy, attention order, media logic, rhythm, interaction
vocabulary, and visual language into dependent screens. Companion screens may
change composition and asset use as their work changes; consistency means that
shared roles and relationships still belong to the same product, not that every
screen repeats one layout or motif.
